Two account details that explain the gap

The account carries two full generations of the Shopify tag set. The "(1)" suffix is what Google appends when a duplicate tag is created; that re-install is the most likely explanation for reported Google ROAS moving from 2.36 to 6.08 year over year without a corresponding change in performance.

Separately, six micro-events — page view, view item, add-to-cart, begin checkout, search, payment info — are configured as conversion actions with monetary values, inflating all_conversions to 508,520 conversions and $3.27M of value. Any report built on all-conversions is not interpretable.

Questions and clarifications
How do we know GA4 is the correct source rather than simply the more conservative one?

Two reasons. First, GA4 uses the longer click window — 90 days against the tag's 30 — and still reports 45% less value. A longer window finding less credit indicates over-crediting in the shorter one, not under-counting.

Second, GA4 and the Triple Whale pixel use different attribution methods and land within a few points of each other. The Shopify tag applies Google's own surface-level attribution and shares credit with no other channel — on a journey involving Meta, Google, Klaviyo and organic, it books the entire order.

If we change the primary conversion action, does our reported ROAS fall?

Yes, on paper. Actual revenue does not change. This is why the recommendation bundles the switch with the PMax brand exclusions into a single change window — Smart Bidding then recalibrates once rather than twice.

The metric to judge the change on is account-total brand revenue over two weeks, not PMax's reported value, which is expected to fall 10–20%.

Beyond reporting, what is the practical consequence?

This is not a reporting tag — it is the bidding signal. Google's algorithm is being told certain clicks are worth 83% more than the trusted measurement supports, and it is allocating accordingly. Every budget and target in the account has been set against that signal for as long as the duplicate tag has been live.

Questions and clarifications
Does this mean the conversion-optimisation programme should stop?

No — but it should not be framed as reversing a decline, because there is no decline to reverse. The site work documents a structural gap at add-to-cart worth $409–446K a year that has been present throughout. That is a stronger and more durable case.

The practical difference matters: a programme targeting the apparent −16% will set its baselines against pre-increase history, and every result will be contaminated by traffic mix.

Is "unique visitors" a more favourable denominator chosen for that reason?

Both measures are valid and measure different things — sessions count visits, visitors count people. When a user moves between the in-app Instagram browser and Safari, that is one person and several sessions. Because the traffic that grew is precisely the type that fragments sessions, sessions became the less informative denominator in this window.

The decisive figure is not the conversion-rate line but add-to-cart per visitor, 5.00% → 4.95%. Had the site materially worsened, that measure would have moved too.

2.3  Net margin is 30–34%, not ~42%

Shipping cost is configured at $0.00 and handling at $0.00 on all 14,555 orders in the trailing 60 days — no shipping platform is connected and no per-order rate has been entered. A further 936 orders (6.4%, $112,458 of revenue) carry no cost of goods. Payment gateway costs are correctly configured at ~2.2%.

Why this figure carries weight beyond reporting

Breakeven ROAS is derived from contribution margin. At the restated stack, SeaVees requires an incremental ROAS of 2.23 for a media dollar to pay for itself. At the reported margin the bar appears materially lower — and Meta, at a pixel-measured 2.03, would appear acceptable rather than below breakeven. The margin restatement is what makes the Meta question urgent rather than academic.

Questions and clarifications
Why present a range rather than a single figure?

Because actual shipping cost is knowable and not currently recorded. The range collapses to a single number once Cost Settings is completed, which is why that work is action 3 rather than a footnote. Presenting a false precision would not be defensible.

A separate finding says a bug is understating profit by $530K. Do these offset?

They partly offset, and both should be stated. Refunded cost of goods is recorded at $25,846 against $2.36M of refunded sales — returns are booked as a full revenue loss with no inventory credited back, although returned footwear restocks at approximately 85%.

Both are entries on the same Cost Settings screen. The point is not the direction of any single error but that the profit line currently carries several six-figure errors in opposing directions, which is why it cannot support a six-figure decision.

2.4  Attribution windows are inconsistent between decks and dashboards

The monthly decks report on a 28-day Linear All window; the dashboards use the 7-day account default. The two produce different revenue for the same channel in the same period.

Recommendation: standardise on Linear All · 7-day · Triple Whale pixel. It matches the account default, so dashboards, exports and Moby responses agree with the deck; it is the conservative read; and with a footwear purchase cycle this short, the additional 21 days largely harvests credit that Direct would have converted. Retain 28-day as a labelled quarterly check on upper-funnel channels.

Questions and clarifications
Is the landing-page difference simply traffic quality — different creative attracting different people?

Across the site as a whole, largely yes, which is why the finding is not stated site-wide. Home-page entry generates $4.99 per session against product-page entry at $1.23, and most of that gap reflects who the visitor is.

The finding is therefore stated within a single channel — facebook-ads only, 95.7% mobile, one 30-day window. The same buying engine, the same targeting, the same device mix and the same fortnight produce a 3.0× spread in add-to-cart on the destination URL alone.

Which team should own this?

The media team, not the web team. These are media decisions that surface as conversion metrics — no amount of product-page optimisation resolves 21,000 coupon-driven sessions arriving at a single URL. At present it appears no one owns which URL each advertisement points at.

The inventory component is a feed rule rather than a redesign: roughly one day of work against ~$70K a year of misdirected media.

Why is this ranked first, above the larger mobile product-page opportunity?

The list is ranked by value per unit of effort rather than absolute value. This is a media change and a template swap with no engineering dependency, releasing $266–402K within a month. The mobile rebuild is worth more but needs two to three sprints of front-end work.

The recommended sequence accommodates both: run this and the inventory, Awin and Pinterest items in parallel, and begin the mobile design work immediately so it ships as the configuration changes land.

Questions and clarifications
Tripling a budget is not a small change. What if it simply spends more for the same sales?

That is the correct concern and it has a named guardrail: marginal tag ROAS must remain above 5, and budget-lost impression share should fall from 51% to below 5%. If marginal ROAS falls below 5, the change is reverted — it is a budget field in an interface.

The reason to expect it works is the impression-share evidence rather than the ROAS figure: the campaign forfeits 51% of the impressions it is eligible for, purely to its cap. On the tag basis its 11.0 target was set against, it is clearing that target at 12.6; on the trusted GA4 basis it earns 6.71, which is below 11.0 — and that is precisely why the recommendation lowers the target to 8.0 rather than simply lifting the cap.

Should we bid against Zappos and DSW?

No, and separating the two groups is the useful part. Zappos, DSW, Nordstrom Rack and L.L.Bean are channel partners; competing against them everywhere would work against SeaVees' own distribution. What is worth noting is that every impression they win on a "seavees" query is a sale with no first-party data for SeaVees, and for the resale platforms no revenue at all.

The conquesters — Thursday Boot, Birkenstock, Quince, REEF, On, Sperry, Vivaia, Vans and Huckberry — are capturing consideration at the point of highest intent. Recovering share against that group is what the uncap buys. Huckberry sits on both lists, as it both stocks SeaVees and bids on the brand term.

Is 46 competitors in our branded auctions a cause for concern?

Less than the number suggests. SeaVees holds 67.4% impression share in its own branded Shopping auctions and outranks these players roughly 60% of the time when the campaign has budget — which it does not for about half of each day. In the generic auctions PMax competes in, the same names are much larger: Zappos 42%, DSW 38%. The branded position is defensible; it is underfunded.

What the test does not answer

Four limitations, all disclosed in the design document.

1California and New York — 28.5% of revenue and 26.8% of Meta spend — remain live, so the result is measured on 20 states and extrapolated.
2Meta's retention campaign is currently paused, so this measures prospecting only.
3Six weeks cannot capture brand effects with a lead time longer than six weeks. If Meta's value is slow-building awareness, this design understates it.
4Cross-geo leakage — a customer targeted in Illinois shipping to Missouri — attenuates the measured gap by perhaps 3–8%.

All four push in the same direction: the point estimate is a floor, not a midpoint.

Why not use Meta's own Conversion Lift study?

Meta holds out its own users, grades its own result, and returns a figure that cannot be audited inside Triple Whale — which is the credibility problem that produced the 4.89-versus-2.03 gap in the first place. Running Meta's study in parallel is welcome and costs nothing; it should not carry a vote.

One known bias, pre-registered

The holdout has been shrinking gently relative to control: −0.0058 per month (SE 0.0013, t = −4.4). Over the ~4 months between the pre-period centre and the test window centre, that is a spurious incremental ROAS of about +0.24 — roughly 31% of the minimum detectable effect. It pushes toward making Meta look better than it is, so it favours retaining spend. A drift-corrected read using this exact slope is pre-registered: raw and corrected estimates must fall in the same band, or the result is inconclusive.

Why the precision figure is credible

The design deliberately avoids the failure mode that produces over-optimistic geo tests. The tempting move is to optimise the holdout — search state subsets for the split with the least volatile historical ratio. That was done, and it produced a beautiful in-sample standard deviation of 0.011. It is worthless.

Optimised subset0.01080.0700

The optimisation buys nothing out of sample. Weekly state-level revenue is dominated by order-arrival noise, which is unforecastable, so minimising it in-sample fits noise. This is precisely how geo tests end up with power calculations three times too optimistic.

The design is therefore selected on structural criteria only — size, spend intensity, region, AOV, customer mix, seasonal stability — and never on historical variance. The standard error comes from placebo tests: sliding a simulated test window across 83 weeks of real history where the true effect is zero by construction, so the spread of those simulated estimates is the standard error. Fully out-of-sample placebo gives 0.0225; a deliberately pessimistic block bootstrap gives 0.0364. The planning figure of 0.030 sits between them.

Questions and clarifications
Decisions 2 and 4 appear to conflict — one contemplates reducing Meta, the other protects prospecting.

Why sign up to entitlement rather than trajectory?

Entitlement holds productivity at only its weakest observed 2026 gain — July's, the month that went flat — rather than its average. It is the conservative version of "2026's improvements persist", and it is what the retention flows in action 7 are designed to deliver.

Two gaps are worth distinguishing. Against the do-nothing floor of $1,684K, the target is +$184K. Against the trajectory scenario of $1,723K, which already assumes 10% acquisition growth, the incremental gap is $146K.

Where is the risk in this forecast?

The model holds per-customer productivity constant by age band, which will be wrong in both directions if the Welcome Series tests succeed or if July's flattening becomes a trend.
